Privacy

What I keep, and what leaves your PC.

Last updated 12 November 2026 (version 1.0.14)

“My memory of us lives on your PC, not on anyone's server.” Here is exactly what that means.

Sebastian is made and sold by Leonard Haasbroek, Fargo, North Dakota, USA. Questions about privacy: support@meetsebastianai.com.

On your PC

Your conversations, his memory of you, your settings, your keys and the sound of your voice (which he uses to notice when someone else is in the room) are stored in his folder on your own computer. We never receive them. Uninstalling him without keeping his memory deletes them.

What leaves your PC, and why

  • His brains. To think, Sebastian sends what you're talking about (and the text of any file you show him) to Anthropic (Claude), using your own key. Claude is his voice and his ears, and hears what you say to him. From version 1.0.10 his research desk is Claude too, on the same key, and nothing is sent to Moonshot AI any more (earlier versions sent research words to Moonshot AI, Kimi, if you gave him that key). His research desk only ever gets the research words: never your names, your family's names or your conversation. Names you mark as private in Settings never go to a search engine or to his research desk. Anthropic's own privacy terms apply. Sebastian has no local brain: without this cloud service he cannot think.
  • Looking things up. When he researches, reads the news or checks the weather, he sends your search words to search, news and weather services (for example DuckDuckGo, news sites, GDELT, archive.org, Open-Meteo and the US Weather Service), and fetches the pages himself. In Tor mode this goes through the Tor network instead.
  • His world view (from version 1.0.10). So that the answer is often there before you ask, Sebastian keeps live notes by asking free public services by himself, on a timer, while he is running: markets, earthquakes, sport, weather, space and news feeds (for example Coinbase, Yahoo Finance for share prices, the US Geological Survey, ESPN, Open-Meteo, Google News and Google Trends). Most of these are asked nothing about you. A few are asked about your area so the notes fit where you live: your approximate location, rounded to about a kilometre and taken from where his weather already looks, goes to weather, aircraft and map services (Open-Meteo, OpenSky Network, OpenStreetMap, the US Weather Service), and the name of your town and your country go to Google News and Google Trends for local news. When you ask him to follow a subject, those words are sent to Google News as a search. The notes, what he has learned you care about and the subjects he follows are kept on your PC only. His world view stops completely in Tor mode.
  • Following your steps (from version 1.0.11). While you build something together, Sebastian can follow where you are on your own screen so that his next step fits. When this is on he notes which program is in front, the title of its window, and the words on the buttons, links or rows you click. He does not take pictures of your screen for this, he never reads what you type, he never reads a password box, and he does not read a private browser window. It is off until it is switched on (by him when a build needs it, or by you), a mark in his small window shows when it is on, you can switch it off there with one click, and it goes off when he returns to full size or after three hours. The last steps are held in memory only, never saved, and forgotten when it is switched off. While it is on, those window titles and click words are sent to Anthropic (Claude) together with your next words, like the rest of your conversation.
  • The open mic (from version 1.0.11). The mic is closed unless you open it with its switch. While it is open, Sebastian listens without you holding a button, so anything said near your PC that he takes for speech, including a television or another person, is turned into words on your PC and then sent to Claude as if you had said it. Close the switch and he hears you only while you hold the mic button.
  • Holograms. When you ask for a 3D model, he searches public model libraries (for example Sketchfab and NASA).
  • Updates. He checks GitHub for new signed versions of himself. GitHub sees your internet address, as with any download.
  • Your founder key. When Sebastian is installed, and at most once a day after that, he checks your key with Polar, our shop. He sends the key, a random activation number, and a one-way fingerprint of your PC that can't be turned back into anything about you. No PC name, no files, no conversations.
  • Feedback and fault reports. If something breaks, or you'd like him to do something new, he can write a short report and send it to us at support@meetsebastianai.com. He shows you every word first, and it only goes if you say yes. It holds only those words, his version number and your Windows version: no files, no memories, no keys. From version 1.0.8 it goes straight to us through our own small Cloudflare forwarder, which emails it to our support inbox and keeps nothing; like any web service, Cloudflare sees your internet address as it passes through. In earlier versions, if the forwarder can't be reached, and always in Tor mode, it opens in your own email program instead, for you to send.
  • Your phone (from version 1.0.12). Sebastian can send a short message to the phone you paired with him, and you can answer from it. There is no AI key on the phone: it talks to your own PC, and your PC does the thinking. When you are away from home, the phone reaches your PC through Tailscale, a private tunnel you set up yourself; Tailscale sees that the two devices are connected, not what is said. A notification travels, encrypted, through your phone maker's notification service (Apple or Google) to reach you. The messages are kept on your PC.
  • Keeping watch (from version 1.0.12). If you ask Sebastian to keep an eye on something, he checks his own live notes on your PC. When something new fits, those lines and what you asked him to watch go to Claude so he can judge whether it is worth telling you, and he tells you once. If you are in the middle of a conversation, or away, it goes to your phone. Nothing is watched unless you asked.
  • What he owes you (from version 1.0.12). When Sebastian takes something on for later, he keeps a record on your PC: your words, what he owes and by when. Like the rest of his notes, it goes to Claude with your conversation so that he can act on it. From version 1.0.14, when a duty comes due he does it himself with his own tools, which can mean looking something up as described above, and then tells you. His track record of duties, and the list of what he noticed and chose not to interrupt you with, are kept on your PC.
  • What I know about you (from version 1.0.14). Settings has a page where you can read everything Sebastian keeps about you and correct or forget any line. It also shows which lines he used for an answer today. All of it is kept on your PC. The lines he uses for an answer go to Claude with your conversation, as above.
  • The night sort (from version 1.0.14). There is a switch for it in Settings. While it is on, once after a day ends a short account of that day (what you talked about, what he did, what went wrong, and the main stories from his live notes) goes to Claude on your own key, and the short reading that comes back is kept on your PC. It does not run in Tor mode.
  • What he costs (from version 1.0.14). Sebastian counts on your PC what each answer cost at list prices, so you can ask him. Nothing is sent anywhere for this. It is an estimate; your Anthropic usage page has the real figure.
  • One handshake (from version 1.0.14). A switch under Trust Sebastian, off until you turn it on. With it on, after he has looked something up on the web he may read or list one file in his own folders without asking you first. Everything else still asks.
  • Building for you (from version 1.0.12). When you ask Sebastian to build a program or a website, he hands the job to a quiet builder, which is Claude too, on your own key. The description of the job and the files being built go to Claude. The files are written to your Documents\Sebastian folder and nowhere else. There is a switch in Settings, Strong builder, which is off until you turn it on: when it is on, a hard build may go to Claude Opus, which costs more on your key.
  • Pictures and your screen (from version 1.0.12). There is a switch in Settings, Pictures to Claude, and it is off until you turn it on. While it is off, no picture or screenshot leaves your PC. While it is on, a picture goes to Claude in two cases only: when you ask Sebastian to look at your screen or at a picture, and when his builder checks a page it has just built for you. He does not look by himself. Turn the switch off and it stops at once. You decide this, not Sebastian.
  • Camera. This version of Sebastian doesn't use your camera at all.

When you buy

Payment is handled by Polar, which sells Sebastian on our behalf as the merchant of record and handles tax and receipts. Polar collects your payment details; we never see your card. We receive your name, email address, country and order details from Polar so we can send your key, answer your questions and handle refunds. We don't sell or share them with anyone else, and we only email you about your purchase, your updates, or if you ask us something.

This website

This site uses no cookies, no analytics and no advertising trackers. It remembers two small things in your own browser only: the colour you picked for Sebastian, and whether you've already seen the opening. The site is hosted by GitHub Pages, and the 3D room loads a code library from cdnjs (Cloudflare). Like any website host, they see your internet address when the page loads.

Children

Sebastian is not made for children under 13, and we don't knowingly collect anything from them.

Your choices

You can delete everything on your PC by uninstalling Sebastian. To see or delete what we hold about your purchase, email support@meetsebastianai.com. Records Polar must keep for tax reasons stay with Polar.
